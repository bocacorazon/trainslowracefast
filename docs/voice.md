When I discovered cycling, it did not take long to realize I had talent somewhat above average. I liked to test it on every ride. I liked to push the pace, push myself, end up feeling like I was going to collapse. I made very quick progress. But at one point I realized I was not catching up to the fast guys. There was always this moment on a very hard ride when it became a bit harder, and I did not have that extra bit. I was at my limit. I also started to notice how tired I always was, and that hopping on the bike seemed like a chore. My indoor training sessions were exhausting and very mentally draining.
Then I came across a book, 80/20 running, where he says that the only training variable that showed significant, general correlation with increased performance was volume. Not intensity, not the nature of the training, but pure volume. And the big realization after that: you can have intensity or you can have volume. Not both. That practical recipe hides the actual mechanism by which the magic happens: increased mitochondria, blah, blah, blah. You might care or you might not. We can point you to the sources if you are. But the truth of the matter is that if you want to improve you must run, ride, ski, row A LOT. And in order to run, ride, ski or row a lot you must not be tired, you must not be injured. A hard week will take more than it gives, if not done with judgement. We can help you with those.
