# Landing Page — Copy v1

*Purpose:* the LP is the exp-1 demand instrument. It tests whether the Convert ICP's wedge ("Easy days, done right") generates measurable demand at the cheapest possible cost, before any product is built.

*Voice & posture:* calm, disciplined, lightly literary. The Convert reads — Maffetone, San Millán, Seiler. Copy should feel like an essay, not a pitch. No exclamation marks. No "unlock your potential." No bro register.

*Copy density:* the LP is read top-to-bottom by curious converts and skimmed by the merely curious. Headlines must carry the message alone; body must reward reading.

## Section 1 — The problem

> ## You signed up to build aerobic base.
> ## Your watch keeps pushing you to 90%.
>
> Most training tools were built around one number: how hard you trained. They reward stress, color your week by intensity, and quietly nudge you out of zone 2 and into the gray zone — where progress comes slowly and injury comes fast.
>
> If you've read the literature, you know your easy days are supposed to be easy. Eighty percent of your weekly hours, genuinely aerobic. Long, repeatable, boring on purpose. The problem is your training tools don't agree. They were built for a different athlete, chasing a different number.
>
> We built this for the converts. The athletes who want to spend their easy days actually easy, log the work honestly, and trust the long arc of training rather than chase next week's TSS.

---

## Section 2 — The wedge

> ## Five honest numbers. No stress score. No readiness score.
>
> We don't show you a TSS. We don't show you a CTL / ATL / TSB triple. We don't show you an FTP estimate or a watch-derived VO2max. We don't paint your week green or red. We don't tell you you're "ready to train hard."
>
> We show you five things, and only these:

| Metric | What it actually means |
|---|---|
| **Adherence %** | How many of your easy minutes were actually easy. Stops and walks excluded — no gaming. |
| **Drift** | How many minutes per week you spent above your easy-day cap. Where the leak is. |
| **Aerobic Trend** | Your pace (or power) at sub-cap heart rate, plotted across the last 14 days. The number that should slowly go up. |
| **Recovery delta** | A descriptive read on how your morning HRV compares to your baseline. Words, not a score. |
| **Weekly distribution** | How much time you spent easy, threshold, and hard. Is your week actually polarized — or are you living in the middle? |

> Every Sunday morning, you get a five-minute review of the last seven days. Built around one question: *did you train slow this week so you can race fast later?*

## Section 3 — Trust

> ## Your data, on your terms.
>
> - **Yours, always.** One-click export. Granular delete. We don't sell data to anyone, anywhere, ever.
> - **No medical claims.** This is a training tool, not medical advice. We say so on every page that needs it.
> - **No readiness theater.** No green / yellow / red signals dressed up as science. We don't think that's honest, and we don't ship it.

## Design tokens (decisions to make at LP build, not now)

The visual identity should reinforce the wedge. The Convert is moving *away* from the typical training-app aesthetic; the LP should look and feel different from a TrainingPeaks / Strava / Garmin Connect surface. Recommended direction:

| Token | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Color palette** | Off-white background, near-black text, single muted accent (suggest: a desaturated forest green or a deep sand). No gradients, no neon. | The "no zone-color soup" stance has a visual implication. |
| **Typography** | Serif headline + sans-serif body. Suggested pair: *Source Serif Pro* + *Inter*. Or *EB Garamond* + *Inter* for a more literary register. | Conveys "read this carefully" rather than "scan this dashboard." |
| **Imagery** | One hero asset: a clean product mockup. Optional secondary: a single black-and-white photograph of a runner on a quiet road, no race-bib energy. | Calm, not aspirational-bro. |
| **Whitespace** | Generous. Section spacing 96–128px. Body line-height 1.6+. | The whole point is "this is different from your watch app." |

## Hero asset spec (for the freelancer brief)

- **Asset:** mock-up of a Sunday Weekly Review email, viewed on either a phone screen or a laptop screen.
- **Content shown in the mockup:**
  - Salutation: "Hi Marcos — here's your week."
  - Five-metric block, vertical:
    - "Adherence: 86% of your easy minutes were actually easy"
    - "Drift: you spent 14 minutes above your cap"
    - "Aerobic Trend: 8:42/mi at 138 bpm — 6 sec/mi faster than two weeks ago"
    - "Recovery: your morning readings tracked your baseline this week"
    - "Distribution: 84% easy / 5% threshold / 11% hard"
  - One line of plain text below: "Easy day Tuesday went a bit hot — see if you can settle that next week."
- **Style:** monochrome with one accent color, serif headline, sans-serif body, very generous whitespace. Not a dashboard. Not a chart explosion. Looks like an editorial newsletter.
- **Format:** PNG + Figma source.
- **Budget:** $300–500. Sources: Dribbble (search "minimal newsletter mockup"), Fiverr Pro, or Figma + a single AI-assisted illustration.

## What's deliberately *not* on this LP

- No pricing. We're testing demand for the wedge, not price sensitivity. Price discovery happens in exp-2.
- No feature roadmap. Roadmaps lock us into commitments we haven't validated.
- No "Why us" / founder-bio section. Adds noise, doesn't move conversion at this stage.
- No social proof block. We don't have any yet, and fake social proof violates the trust posture.
- No "Compare to TrainingPeaks / Strava" table. The wedge does that work in prose; a comparison table reads as combative.
- No newsletter / blog / content marketing surface. exp-1 is a single-page test.
- No login link. Nothing to log into yet.

## Open questions for founder review

1. **Cohort timing language.** "Later this year" is vague. Are we comfortable with that, or do we want a quarter ("late summer," "Q4")? Vague is safer pre-build; specific is more compelling. Recommend keeping "later this year" until we have build dates.
2. **Brand name on the LP.** "trainslowracefast" reads as a wordmark; consider whether to set it lowercase / sentence-case / typographic. This is a design decision but should be consistent with the domain.
3. **The "indie team" line in the footer.** Honest, slightly disarming, but does it undercut credibility for the Convert who is choosing between us and an incumbent? Alternative: "Built by athletes for athletes." (More common, but more bro.) Recommend keeping "indie team" — the Convert tends to value craft over scale.
4. **Calendly soft CTA after waitlist signup.** Currently routes only respondents who selected an aerobic-method-related answer. Should we route everyone? Recommend keeping the routing — the screener is doing exp-2 candidate qualification work.
5. **The "talk to your physician" footer line.** Defensible posture per W5 / W6. Consider whether to make it slightly warmer ("if you're unsure, talk to your doctor") vs. clinical ("talk to your physician before starting any training program"). Lawyer review at Phase 5 will lock the exact wording; for v1 the clinical version is correct.
