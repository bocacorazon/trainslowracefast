# Brand voice guide

Source of truth: `docs/01_demand_positioning_workshop.md` (W1) and `docs/05_brand_workshop.md` (W5).

## Voice posture: engineer-honest

Direct. Precise. Uses concrete numbers. Comfortable with technical specificity.
Founder is an engineer, not a writer. The voice should reflect that authentically
rather than mimic literary or influencer registers.

## Do

- State numbers and units precisely. ("82% Adherence over 8 weeks", not "great results").
- Use plain technical language. ("HR drift", "aerobic threshold", "MAF cap").
- Acknowledge tradeoffs and uncertainty when real. ("we don't yet know whether...").
- Quote athletes verbatim where possible.
- Keep paragraphs short. Use lists for parallel items.

## Don't

- "Unlock your potential", "transform your training", "the secret to...", "game-changer".
- Exclamation marks.
- Stacks of adjectives ("incredible, life-changing, revolutionary").
- Implied medical claims ("cure", "fix", "heal", "guarantee").
- Generic LLM tells: "delve", "in the realm of", "it's important to note", "as an AI".
- Marketing fluff that could be said about any fitness product.

## Reference points (positive)

- Intervals.icu's blog posts: technical, specific, founder voice.
- Hutchinson's "Endure": evidence-driven, measured, comfortable with nuance.
- Stephen Seiler's interviews: precise, willing to say "we don't know yet".

## Reference points (negative)

- Most TrainingPeaks marketing copy.
- Influencer Instagram captions.
- Generic SaaS landing pages ("Built for athletes who want to win.").

## Format conventions

- Headlines under 12 words.
- Subhead numbers when promising specifics ("8-week concierge cohort", "Five metrics").
- Disclaimers in plain language, not legalese, where the legalese isn't load-bearing.
- Compliance language goes in a footer or trust page, not in body copy.
